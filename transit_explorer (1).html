Learn · Understand

Formulae & Proof

Build the idea first, then connect it to the formula.

01 · Coordinates

The Cartesian plane

Two perpendicular number lines locate every point using an ordered pair (x, y). The x-coordinate is read first, then the y-coordinate.

P(x, y) = (horizontal position, vertical position)
Example: P(4, −2) is 4 units right and 2 units down from the origin.
02 · Distance

Distance formula

The horizontal and vertical differences form the legs of a right triangle. Pythagoras gives the straight-line distance.

d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]

Example: A(0, 0), B(3, 4) → d = √(9 + 16) = 5 units.

03 · Midpoint

Midpoint formula

Average the x-coordinates to find the horizontal position of the halfway point. Do the same with the y-coordinates.

M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)

Example: A(2, 4), B(6, 10) → M(4, 7).

04 · Quadrants

Read the signs

I: (+,+) · II: (−,+)
III: (−,−) · IV: (+,−)

Quadrants are counted counter-clockwise, starting at the top-right. A point on an axis is not inside a quadrant.

Remember: the origin is (0, 0). It is where both axes intersect.

Try a worked example

Find the distance and midpoint between A(−3, 2) and B(5, −4).

Δx = 5 − (−3) = 8; Δy = −4 − 2 = −6
d = √(8² + (−6)²) = √100 = 10 units
M = ((−3 + 5)/2, (2 − 4)/2) = (1, −1)

Culture & Connections

Connect coordinate geometry to the people, languages, places and subjects that shape transport. Mathematics is shared across communities, but each city has its own stories and travel needs.

CULTURE & PLACE

Compare journeys across communities

Compare local trains in Mumbai, ferries in coastal communities, buses in hill towns and walking routes near schools. The transport mode may differ, but coordinates can still represent locations and distance.

Consider geography, cost, accessibility, climate and community needs. Avoid assuming everyone in a community travels the same way.
MATHEMATICS + GEOGRAPHY

Map scale and location

A coordinate grid models relative positions. Real maps also need a scale, orientation and geographic reference system. Grid distance is not automatically actual road or rail distance.

Model distance ≠ always actual travel distance
MATHEMATICS + SCIENCE

Energy and environment

The Green Mobility calculator compares estimated emissions. Science explains energy and emissions; mathematics supports multiplication, comparison and interpreting model assumptions.

Estimated impact = distance × commuters × assumed factor
MATHEMATICS + SOCIAL SCIENCE

Fair access to transport

A midpoint may help identify a meeting point, but fairness also depends on safety, travel time, fares, disability access and service frequency. The central point is not always the most accessible point.

MATHEMATICS + LANGUAGE

Explain a route clearly

Describe a journey using coordinates, then explain it in a language you are comfortable with. Partners can compare the mathematical meaning even when the words differ.

Activity: Start at (−2, 3), move to (4, 3), then to (4, −1). Sketch the route and explain the horizontal and vertical movements.
